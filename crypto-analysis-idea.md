# Crypto Analysis Idea

## Core Analysis Flow

**Problem → Product → Users / Customers → Adoption → Revenue → Token Demand → Value Accrual → Buyback / Burn → Supply → Sustainability / Competition → Valuation**

## 1. Problem & Project Idea
- What real problem does the project solve?
- Is there genuine demand for the solution?
- Does the product actually need blockchain or a token?
- A good idea alone is not enough; look for real utility and product-market fit.

## 2. Product
- What product or service does the project provide?
- Would people still want to use the product even without token incentives?
- Separate genuine product usage from speculative token activity.

## 3. Users / Customers & Market
- Who are the real customers?
- Can economic activity develop among Consumer ↔ Consumer, Business ↔ Consumer, or Business ↔ Business participants?
- A healthy ecosystem should have real exchange/activity, not only token trading.

## 4. Adoption
- Is the product reaching and retaining real users?
- Do not confuse token distribution or airdrop wallets with product adoption.
- Useful indicators can include active users, transaction volume, retention, fees/revenue, and TVL where relevant.

## 5. Revenue
- How does the project make money?
- Is revenue sustainable and generated by real product/service usage?
- Distinguish real revenue from token emissions, treasury spending, or temporary incentives.

## 6. Token Demand
- Why does anyone need to own or use the token?
- Does product growth create additional token demand?
- Examples: payment/service access, staking, collateral, governance, fee utility.

## 7. Value Accrual
Project success does not automatically mean token-holder success.

Analyze how value generated by the project reaches the token:
- Fees → Token holders
- Revenue → Buyback
- Fees → Burn
- Staking/value distribution
- Required token utility

## 8. Buyback / Burn
- Buyback should not be assumed or treated as an unlimited guarantee.
- Identify exactly where the money for buybacks comes from.
- Prefer sustainable mechanisms funded by real revenue/fees.
- Treasury-funded buybacks without sustainable revenue may eventually run out.

Example:

**Users → Fees → Protocol Revenue → Buyback/Burn → Token Value Accrual**

## 9. Supply
Scarcity alone does not create value.

Analyze:
- Circulating supply
- Maximum/total supply
- Inflation/emissions
- Token unlock schedules
- Burn rate
- Insider/team/investor allocations

Key principle:

**Demand Growth > Effective Supply Growth**

A shrinking supply is useful only when meaningful demand exists.

## 10. Sustainability & Competition
- Can the business model survive without continuously subsidizing users?
- What competitors solve the same problem?
- Does the project have a moat, network effect, liquidity advantage, technology advantage, brand, or ecosystem advantage?

## 11. Valuation
After understanding the fundamentals, compare them with:
- Market cap
- FDV
- Revenue/fees
- Supply and future unlocks
- Market share
- Comparable projects

Do not conclude that a strong project automatically means an undervalued token. Product success and token economics must be analyzed separately.

---

## Main Principle

**Real Problem → Useful Product → Real Users → Sustainable Revenue → Token Demand → Token Value Accrual**

Then evaluate **Supply + Competition + Sustainability + Valuation** before reaching an investment thesis.
